Tools

Overview

Senior design capstone. The system is a solid-motor launch vehicle and a fixed-wing UAV with folding wings, carried inside the airframe and released in flight.

Current work covers preliminary vehicle configuration, wing-deployment concepts, and the mechanical and avionics interfaces between the rocket and the UAV.

Phase 1Preliminary design

My contributions

  • Developed preliminary CAD models and OpenRocket simulations for the launch vehicle and deployable UAV, evaluating packaging constraints, vehicle configuration, flight stability, and rocket-aircraft integration.
  • Investigated folding-wing deployment concepts and mechanical coupling methods for securing and releasing the UAV.
  • Defined preliminary mechanical and avionics integration requirements: flight-computer packaging, deployment interfaces, control-surface configuration for flight and roll stability, and overall subsystem layout.

Overview

My contributions

Overview

Recovery subsystem for SEDS UNLV's IREC 2026 rocket, using a redundant dual-deploy architecture. Led system design, testing, integration, and documentation, coordinating with the avionics, structures, and propulsion teams.

The avionics sled carries two independent dual-deploy altimeters and a Featherweight GPS tracker. Components can be removed without disassembling the bay, and an integrated screw-switch port controls power once the sled is installed.

8+Engineers on the recovery subsystem team

My contributions

  • Designed a modular avionics sled supporting dual flight computers and GPS in SolidWorks, 3D printed for high-vibration and thermal environments.
  • Developed a MATLAB shock-load calculator to predict parachute deployment loads and drive structural design decisions.
  • Performed ANSYS static FEA on machined aluminum bulkheads for stress, deformation, and factor of safety under worst-case separation loads.
  • Produced the avionics block diagram used in progress reports and design reviews.
  • Managed subsystem documentation, BOMs, and progress reports for flight readiness and configuration control.

Overview

Aramis used a redundant dual-deploy architecture pairing an SRAD Eggtimer Quasar with a COTS Altus Metrum TeleMetrum, with independent apogee and main deployment circuits and live GPS telemetry.

A pre-competition test flight deployed both parachutes nominally. At IREC 2025 the vehicle flew without anomalies and received a 100% recovery score.

100%Recovery score — 10,000 ft COTS division, IREC 2025

±5%Ejection-charge prediction vs. ground test

My contributions

  • Redesigned the recovery avionics sled in SolidWorks and fabricated it by 3D printing; integrated dual altimeters and a camera.
  • Built an Excel black-powder charge calculator that predicted ejection forces within ±5% of experimental results.
  • Handled avionics integration and soldering, and ran OpenRocket flight simulations for dual-deploy performance.
  • Analyzed test-flight telemetry to evaluate parachute deployment, ejection timing, and overall flight performance.
